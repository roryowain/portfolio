Where the time actually goes

Four places it shows up on most jobs, with the arithmetic behind each shown honestly — not promised.

Tender & framework submissions

Now: every ITT starts from a blank document — method statements, pricing schedules, CVs and policies pulled from old bids and reformatted by hand, usually by your best estimator.

Instead: a maintained library of clauses, CVs and policies assembles a first-pass submission your team reviews and finishes, rather than rebuilds from scratch.

Illustrative: 4 submissions/month × ~1.5 days released × ~£400/day loaded ≈ £2,400/month

Subcontractor & supplier invoices

Now: delivery notes and applications for payment get rekeyed into the accounting system line by line, then chased when the numbers don't match.

Instead: invoices and delivery notes are read and matched against the order automatically; only genuine mismatches land on someone's desk.

Illustrative: 250 invoices/month × ~7 min released × ~£22/hr loaded ≈ £640/month

Valuations & variation orders

Now: each month's valuation means a QS working back through variation instructions, reconciling them against the original quote, project by project.

Instead: variations are logged against the original quote as they happen, so the valuation pack goes into the meeting already reconciled.

Illustrative: ~2 days/month released across a typical live project list × ~£380/day loaded ≈ £760/month

Site reporting

Now: photos, progress notes and plant/labour figures get written up into a daily or weekly report by hand, usually after the site's gone quiet.

Instead: photos and notes captured through the day are turned into a first-draft report; the site manager checks it and sends it.

Illustrative: 4 site managers × ~20 min/day released × ~£28/hr loaded ≈ £930/month

Illustrative, based on typical figures for a firm this size — not a promise. Your audit prices your own operation, project by project.
