Reasonable objections

FAQ
We've already tried AI and it didn't stick.

That's the normal outcome, and it's usually not a tool problem. Someone tries ChatGPT, gets a good result once, and then nothing changes because no process was rebuilt around it. The 54%-say-yes / 11%-actually-automate gap is exactly this. The audit starts from your processes, not from a tool.

£3,500 is a lot for a document.

It is, which is why it's guaranteed against three times its own fee in annualised savings. If you'd rather test the thinking first, the readiness check is free and takes four minutes, and the call after it costs nothing.

Will this mean laying people off?

In almost every business I look at, no — it means the people you already have stop spending their week on re-keying and chasing. Most owner-managed firms are under-staffed for the work they want to do, not over-staffed for the work they have. If redundancy is your goal, I'm probably not the right person.

Our data is sensitive.

Then that constrains the design, and it's better to say so on day one. Plenty of what pays best runs entirely inside your own systems and never sends data anywhere. The audit flags what's safe, what needs care, and what I'd refuse to do.

Are you going to disappear after the audit?

The audit is deliberately standalone — a roadmap you can hand to anyone, including an internal team or another supplier. If you want me to install it, that's step 4. If you don't, you've still got the document you paid for.

How quickly does anything actually change?

The audit takes two to three weeks. On a retainer, the first thing I build is a dashboard you can see, followed by one automation your staff feel within the first week or two. Deliberately: momentum matters more than the perfect roadmap.
