Where the time actually goes

None of this needs a research lab. It needs someone to sit with your quoting sheet, your goods-in desk and your Friday report, and build the boring bit once.

Quoting and estimating

~£1,300/mo

Now: every enquiry means rebuilding a costed quote from scratch: checking prices, digging out the last similar job, hoping nothing's moved since.

Instead: a quoting tool pulls current costs and past-job pricing automatically, so most quotes start as a finished draft, not a blank spreadsheet.

≈25 quotes/week · 45 min cut to 15 · £24/hr loaded ≈ £1,300/month

Purchase orders & supplier invoices

~£550/mo

Now: the same figures get typed out between email, a spreadsheet and the accounts system, then checked by eye for mismatches.

Instead: line items are pulled straight from the PO or invoice into the accounts system, and mismatches get flagged rather than hunted for.

≈150 invoices/month · 10 min saved each · £22/hr loaded ≈ £550/month

Production & OEE reporting

~£560/mo

Now: someone walks the floor with a clipboard, then loses a chunk of Friday afternoon turning shift data into a report.

Instead: shift data is logged at source and rolls up into a live dashboard: the weekly report writes itself.

≈5 hrs/week compiling recovered · £26/hr loaded ≈ £560/month

Stock & goods-in paperwork

~£600/mo

Now: manual reconciliation between the delivery note, the system and the shelf: every goods-in checked and keyed in by hand.

Instead: scanning at goods-in matches delivery notes to purchase orders automatically and updates stock in real time.

≈200 deliveries/month · 9 min saved each · £20/hr loaded ≈ £600/month

Illustrative: every figure above assumes a role, a volume and a loaded hourly cost that won't match your business exactly. The audit prices your own operation, not an average one. Add the four up on a mid-sized site, though, and it's often close to what the audit itself costs, once.
